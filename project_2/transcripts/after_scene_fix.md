**Ada Chen:** Actually, I'd reframe that. He's not the difficult patient—you're in a difficult *situation*, and he's responding rationally to it. If I were post-op day three, septic, and no one had time to explain what was happening to me, I'd be suspicious too.

The distrust isn't the problem you need to solve. It's the symptom. The actual problem is you don't have enough staffing to do both competent care *and* communication, and you're choosing to resent the patient instead of the system.

Here's what I'd actually do: Stop trying to convince him you're trustworthy. Instead, give him *control*. Let him watch you draw the antibiotics—that's not slowing you down meaningfully. Explain the septic workup in plain language: what you're testing for, why, what the timeline is. Give him a written summary he can reference. Make the daughter part of the solution, not the problem—have her call back at specific update times instead of random hours.

You're documenting to cover yourself, which I understand, but that's defensive. What you should be doing is removing the gaps where his distrust can grow.

Do you actually have five minutes to sit with him once, or is this truly a capacity problem?
