**Marcus Webb:** That's exactly what I'm here for, Jade. No such thing as an obvious question when it comes to health insurance—most people find it confusing, and that's not on you.

HDHP stands for High Deductible Health Plan. The basic difference is this: you pay a lower monthly premium, but your deductible—that's the amount you have to pay out of pocket before the insurance kicks in—is significantly higher than our standard plan. So if you're generally healthy and don't expect to need much care, you're paying less each month. But if something does happen, you're covering more of those costs yourself until you hit that deductible threshold.

The trade-off is that HDHPs come with a Health Savings Account, or HSA, which is actually a nice benefit if you can take advantage of it. You put pre-tax money into that account specifically for medical expenses, and it rolls over year to year. Some people use it strategically—save it up over time and use it for out-of-pocket costs.

So the question is really about your situation. Do you typically use a lot of healthcare, or are you pretty healthy? And do you have the financial cushion to cover a higher deductible if something unexpected happens? That'll tell you whether it makes sense for you.
